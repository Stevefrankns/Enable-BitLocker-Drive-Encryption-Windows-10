How to Enable BitLocker Drive Encryption on Windows 10

BitLocker is Microsoft's powerful, built-in full disk encryption tool. But activating it isn't always straightforward, especially depending on your Windows edition and hardware configuration. Here is the complete guide to securing your drives.

Editorial Team · Updated Oct 2023 · 12 min read
Quick Answer

To enable BitLocker on Windows 10, press the Windows key, type "Manage BitLocker," and select it. Choose the drive you want to encrypt and click Turn on BitLocker. Follow the wizard to choose your backup method for the recovery key and select whether to encrypt the entire drive or only used space. Note: BitLocker is only natively available on Windows 10 Pro, Enterprise, and Education editions.

Before you begin

Requirements and Hardware Tradeoffs

BitLocker drive encryption provides full disk encryption (FDE), meaning everything on your drive—files, the operating system, and temporary data—is secured using the AES algorithm (typically AES-128 or AES-256). Before you attempt to turn on BitLocker, you need to verify two critical things: your Windows edition and your hardware.

TPM Requirement for BitLocker

By default, BitLocker relies on a Trusted Platform Module (TPM) chip on your computer's motherboard. The TPM generates and stores encryption keys. If your system lacks a TPM (common in older custom-built PCs), you will see an error when trying to activate BitLocker. While you can bypass the TPM requirement using Group Policy, doing so means you must insert a USB flash drive containing the startup key every time you boot the computer.

Windows Edition Compatibility Note

The single most common hurdle users face is discovering that BitLocker is not supported on Windows 10 Home. Microsoft limits this feature to Pro, Enterprise, and Education editions.

Native Method

How to Enable BitLocker on Windows 10

If you have Windows 10 Pro and a supported TPM, setting up BitLocker takes only a few minutes. Follow this setup wizard walkthrough.

Step 1: Open BitLocker Management

Open the Start menu and search for "Manage BitLocker." Alternatively, open the Control Panel, navigate to System and Security, and click on BitLocker Drive Encryption.

You will see a list of your drives. Locate your operating system drive (usually C:) or a secondary data drive, and click Turn on BitLocker next to it.

Step 2: Back Up Your Recovery Key

This is the most critical step. If your motherboard fails, or if you update your BIOS, BitLocker may lock you out. You must have a recovery key. Windows will ask how you want to back up your BitLocker recovery key:

  • Save to your Microsoft account (Recommended for most users)
  • Save to a USB flash drive
  • Save to a file (must be on a different drive)
  • Print the recovery key

Step 3: Choose Encryption Amount and Mode

Next, choose whether to encrypt Used disk space only (faster, best for new PCs) or the Entire drive (slower, essential for PCs already in use).

You will then be asked to choose an encryption mode. If the drive is fixed in your Windows 10 PC, choose New encryption mode (XTS-AES). If you are using BitLocker to Go on a removable USB drive that will be used on older Windows versions, choose Compatible mode.

How to Verify It Worked

After completing the wizard, Windows will begin encrypting. You can continue using your PC during this time, though you may notice a slight performance impact on full disk encryption until the process finishes. To verify, open "This PC" in File Explorer. A padlock icon will appear on the encrypted drive.

Troubleshooting BitLocker Errors

BitLocker is deeply integrated into Windows hardware, which means it can be finicky. Here are the most common errors and how to resolve them.

BitLocker Recovery Flowchart

Can't access your drive? Follow these steps.

If the key is permanently lost, Bitlocker data recovery without key is mathematically impossible due to AES-256 encryption. The drive must be formatted.
Common Errors:

"A valid USB key was not detected bitlocker": Ensure the USB containing the startup key is formatted as FAT32, not NTFS.

"TPM is not ready": Boot into your BIOS/UEFI settings and clear the TPM, then restart.

How to Disable BitLocker

If you no longer want encryption or need to update your motherboard BIOS (which will trip the TPM security), you should temporarily or permanently disable it.

  1. Open the Start menu and type "Manage BitLocker."
  2. Find your encrypted drive.
  3. Click Turn off BitLocker (to decrypt entirely) or Suspend protection (to temporarily disable it for a reboot or update).

Our Verdict

BitLocker is an exceptional, enterprise-grade security tool built right into Windows 10 Pro. If you meet the hardware requirements and have a Pro license, it is the best way to secure your entire machine against physical theft.

However, if you are a Windows 10 Home user, or if you share a computer and need to keep specific files private from other logged-in users, full disk encryption is the wrong tool for the job. In those cases, a dedicated file encryption tool is necessary.
